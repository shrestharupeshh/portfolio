Nutrient Timing & Mid-Run Fueling

When you eat determines how your body uses fuel. Strategic timing maximizes performance and recovery.

⏰ Pre-Run Timing

2-3 Hours Before

Full meal — rice + dal, oats, roti + egg. Complex carbs + moderate protein. No high fat, no high fiber (causes GI issues during running).

30-60 Minutes Before

Light snack only if needed — banana, few dates, white toast. Your banana + mandarin 40 min before runs is correct.

⚠️ Avoid: The 15-45 Min Window

Do not eat significant carbs in this window — reactive hypoglycemia risk. Blood sugar spikes then crashes right as you start running.

⚡ Post-Run Recovery Window

Within 30 Minutes (Critical)

3:1 carb-to-protein ratio. Your whey + creatine immediately post-run + full meal within 80 min is correct two-stage approach. This is when muscles are maximally receptive to nutrients.

1-2 Hours Post-Run

Full recovery meal. Rice + protein + cooked vegetables. Glycogen restoration continues for 24-48 hours but is fastest in first 2 hours.

Example: Cookies Mid-Afternoon (Your April 18 Experience)

Sugar rush + crash when inactive = reactive hypoglycemia from simple sugars without exercise to absorb glucose. On rest afternoons, stick to complex carbs + protein snacks.

🏃‍♂️ Mid-Run Fueling — The Glycogen Clock

Your muscles store ~90 min worth of glycogen at easy pace. After that, without external fuel, performance degrades rapidly — this is "the wall." You need 30-60g carbs per hour for runs over 75 min, consumed every 30-45 min. Start fueling before you feel hungry — hunger during run means you're already depleted.

Gut Safety, Antinutrients & Food Optimization

You've spent 18 months restoring your gut from IBS and constipation. Here's how to protect that progress during marathon training.

⚠️ Understanding Antinutrients

Antinutrients are compounds in food that interfere with nutrient absorption. They evolved in plants as protection against being eaten. Not dangerous in normal amounts but can reduce mineral absorption and cause GI discomfort if eaten in excess or certain conditions.

Phytates

Found in: Oats, dal, nuts, rice bran

Effect: Binds iron, zinc, calcium — reduces absorption

Mitigation: Soak oats 30+ min before cooking (reduces 30-50%). Soak and rinse dal before cooking. Yogurt alongside oats helps (fermentation/acid degrades phytates).

Lectins

Found in: Beans, chickpeas, soybean chunks, kidney beans

Effect: Raw lectins cause serious GI distress (nausea, vomiting)

Mitigation: Fully cooked lectins are almost entirely inactivated and safe. Soybean chunks are pre-processed — lectin content low. Chickpeas must be fully cooked, never undercooked. Pressure cooking destroys lectins more completely than boiling.

Oxalates

Found in: Spinach, nuts, cocoa

Effect: Bind calcium, can contribute to kidney stones in susceptible people

Mitigation: Your nut intake (walnuts, almonds) is moderate — no concern at your quantities. If you add spinach to curries, alternate with other greens. Cocoa in oatmeal occasionally is fine.

FODMAPs

Your IBS History

Fermentable carbs causing bloating/distress in IBS-prone people.

High-FODMAP in your diet: Cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli (large amounts), black dal (whole), chickpeas, peanut butter (large amounts)

Safe when: Cooked well. Raw cabbage causes far more bloating than cooked. This explains historical bloating — FODMAP load from raw + dairy together.

🥗 Raw vs Cooked — Your Specific Profile

Cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, green beans, chickpeas, all dal, soybean chunks, tofu (always better cooked — improves protein digestibility)

🦠 Gut Health During Marathon Training

Marathon training creates real gut stress — impact running reduces blood flow to intestines, high training load increases cortisol disrupting gut motility, poor fueling can trigger conditions you've recovered from. Here's how to protect your progress.

Probiotic Maintenance

Continue daily yogurt ✅. Consider adding fermented food — homemade gaadeko achaar (fermented pickle) if you can make it, or store-bought kimchi 1-2 tbsp with lunch twice a week.

These add microbial diversity yogurt alone doesn't cover. Miso paste in warm water as light soup is another accessible option.

Prebiotic Foods — Feed Your Good Bacteria

Oats (daily oatmeal) ✅, slightly underripe banana ✅, cooked and cooled rice (resistant starch forms when rice cools — reheating preserves some), garlic and onion in curries ✅, cooked legumes ✅

Your existing diet is actually prebiotic-rich. Keep it.

Runner's Gut — What It Is

Impact running reduces intestinal blood flow by up to 80% during intense effort. Can cause cramping, urgency, diarrhea — especially at higher intensities.

Prevention: No high-fiber within 2 hrs of running, no high-fat within 3 hrs, stay hydrated, don't increase intensity faster than gut adapts.

If you experience GI distress during runs, slow down — it almost always resolves as intensity drops.

Stress and Gut Connection

Your prostatitis and IBS both have stress-gut axis component. High training load weeks (Phase 3) will increase cortisol, directly affecting gut motility and can trigger flare-ups.

Prioritize sleep in peak weeks not just for running performance but for gut health. Pelvic pain episodes appearing before long runs worth monitoring for this pattern.

🥛 Milk and Lactose — Why It Stopped Working

Lactase enzyme production decreases in many South Asian adults after childhood — genetically common in Nepali and South Asian populations. US dairy often higher in fat and processed differently than what you were used to.

Your decision to quit milk was correct. Yogurt and paneer are lower-lactose dairy options most lactose-sensitive people tolerate well because fermentation partially degrades lactose. Keep yogurt, avoid liquid milk.

Hydration & Supplementation Strategy

Strategic hydration and evidence-based supplements to support performance, recovery, and long-term health.

💧 Daily Hydration Targets

You lose ~0.5-1.5L/hour running depending on temperature and sweat rate. Sacramento in April-July gets warm — dehydration of just 2% body weight reduces performance by 10-20%. By peak summer long runs, heat and dehydration management become as important as training itself.

Daily Baseline

3.0 – 3.5 Liters water/day during training weeks. Urine should be pale yellow — dark yellow = dehydrated, clear = overhydrated (also bad).

Before Runs

Drink 400-600ml in the 2 hours before. Don't chug right before — sip steadily.

After Runs

1.5× whatever you lost. Weigh yourself before/after long run — every 1kg lost = ~1L to replace.

Sacramento Summer Reality

From Week 8 onward (mid-May), start runs before 7am or after 7pm. Running in 35°C+ adds ~20 bpm to HR at any pace — this is physiological, not fitness decline. Always carry water on runs over 8K in summer.

🧂 Electrolytes — Why They Matter

Sweat contains sodium, potassium, magnesium, chloride — not just water. Replacing water without electrolytes during long runs causes hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium), which has hospitalized runners. Especially relevant for runs over 2 hours.

Sodium (Most Important)

Lost heavily in sweat. Add pinch of salt to water bottle on runs over 90 min. Or use electrolyte tabs (Nuun, SIS Go Hydro).

Potassium

Banana before/during long runs. Helps prevent cramping.

Magnesium

Your walnuts, raisins, seeds help. Cramping not fixed by hydration often magnesium deficiency.

Simple Rule

Runs under 75 min: Water only

Over 75 min: Electrolytes every 45-60 min alongside water

Your Day 13 long run (80 min) was right at threshold — add electrolytes from next long run onward.

🚰 Drinking During Runs — How and When

Drinking strategy matters — both when you drink and how much. Overdrinking is also real risk (hyponatremia).

Short Runs (<60 min)

No water needed if well hydrated going in. Bring water once Sacramento summer starts (May onward).

Runs 60-90 min

150-200ml every 20 min. Sip — don't chug. Chugging causes stitch.

Long Runs 90+ min

150-250ml every 15-20 min + electrolytes every 45 min. Carry handheld bottle or hydration vest from Week 7 onward.

How to Drink While Running

Slow to easy jog, don't stop completely. Pinch cup into tube shape (at aid stations), tilt head slightly back. Takes practice — do it at least 3 times in training.

💡 Important Notes on Weight Goals During Training

Bulking During Marathon Training (Not Recommended)

Gaining weight while running high mileage is metabolically difficult and biomechanically inefficient. Extra weight increases joint stress and injury risk. If you must bulk, do it in off-season with reduced running volume and increased strength training.

Cutting/Losing Weight During Marathon Training (Risky)

Calorie deficit during training impairs recovery, increases injury risk, and reduces performance. If you need to lose fat, do it in base-building phases with very conservative deficits (250 kcal/day max). Never cut during peak training weeks.

Best Approach for Most Runners

Maintain weight during marathon training. Focus on body recomposition (lose fat, gain muscle) through proper nutrient timing and strength work. Save intentional weight changes for off-season.

Eat by 18:00-19:00. White rice 1.5-2 cups + mild chicken or dal curry. Cooked vegetables only, small portions. NO avocado, NO peanut butter, NO ice cream. Low fat, low fiber, high carb. Hydrate well but not excessively.

Race Morning (5:15am start)

04:00 — Wake Up
Chiura 80-100g roasted + 2 boiled eggs + 1 banana + water. OR oats 50g + 2 eggs + banana.

04:15-04:30 — Coffee (if using caffeine)
1-2 cups = 180-360mg caffeine

04:45 — Final Hydration
300-400ml water, sip slowly

05:00 — Pre-Start
1 banana or 3-4 dates, minimal water

During Race (42.2K)

Fueling: Gel/dates at km 10, 20, 28, 35, 40 (every ~30-45 min)
Hydration: 150-200ml water every aid station (~2K apart), electrolytes at km 15, 25, 35
If GI distress hits: Slow pace immediately, walk if needed, sip water, skip next fuel if necessary — finish time matters less than finishing safely
